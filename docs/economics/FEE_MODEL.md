# Fee model

Status: working model, 2026-10-12. It answers the instruction in ADR-0010 and the decisions log (2): "final numbers are modelled before mainnet". It does not decide the numbers. Every rate below is a proposal, every volume is an input, and the tables mark the inputs. The tables between the `generated` markers are written by `pnpm --filter @opening-cross/fees model` from the same code that computes fees in the billing ledger, and a test fails if they drift from this file.

## 1. What the model is and is not

It is exact arithmetic: given a schedule, an integrator fee, a mix of cross kinds and a notional, it returns what the program would charge, who receives it and what the protocol keeps after referral payouts and the market-maker rebate pool. The functions are the ones that reproduce 30 of 30 devnet finalize transactions to the atom (`docs/evidence/D-billing-ledger.md`).

It is not a forecast. There is no mainnet volume to extrapolate from; the 48 devnet crosses are our own test traffic and the revenue page says so. It does not know how many issuers will pay, how bidders respond to a fee, or what an integrator will choose to charge. Those are the questions in section 7, and nothing in the proposal should be read as an answer to them.

## 2. The rule being modelled

- A fee is charged only on filled notional. Never on a bid, a cancel or a refund.
- The paying side is the seller when sellers supply the token, and the issuer (out of what buyers paid) when the issuer mints for buyers. A buyer in a cross pays the clearing price and no fee.
- The protocol fee is `min(schedule[kind], floor(integratorBps / 4))`, so it is at most a fifth of the total, and a cross with no integrator fee pays nothing. The integrator fee is 0 to 300 basis points.
- Of the protocol's share: 10% of the part generated by referred orders goes to the referrer; a quarter of the part earned on reopen crosses is reserved for outside market makers (the programme itself is not built). The rest is operating revenue.

## 3. What it costs a participant, against what the alternatives publish

Rates the counterparties publish today, read on 2026-10-12:

| Venue | Rate | Charged on | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| pump.fun bonding curve | 1.25% per trade: 0.30% creator, 0.95% protocol, 0% to liquidity | every trade, buy and sell | pump.fun/docs/fees |
| Meteora DBC | trading fee of at least 0.25% (configurable up to 99%); the protocol takes 20% of it and a referrer 20% of that; plus a fixed 0.2% protocol liquidity fee at migration | every swap, buy and sell | docs.meteora.ag, DBC fees overview |
| Opening Cross, proposed | 30 bps launch, 5 reopen, 15 tender, 30 recurring protocol fee, plus whatever the integrator charges (0 to 300 bps); the protocol share capped at a fifth of the total | the fill, on the paying side only | ADR-0010 |
| MetaDAO launchpad | NOT VERIFIED: no fee schedule could be read from their published documents | | |
| Metaplex Genesis, Uniswap CCA, Raydium LaunchLab | NOT VERIFIED here | | |

An investor who buys and later sells pays 2 x 1.25% = 2.5% of notional on pump.fun's curve. On Opening Cross, with the proposed 30 bps protocol rate and a 120 bps integrator fee, the same investor pays nothing to buy (the issuer pays 150 bps of the raise) and 150 bps on the later sale: 1.5%. That is arithmetic on published rates. It says nothing about what an investor ends up paying in slippage, and an integrator that charges 300 bps changes it. The point it supports is narrow: charging the fill once, on one side, is a lower headline cost than charging every trade, and it is not a reason by itself to expect volume.

Three things the table shows. At zero integrator fee the protocol earns nothing, by design: the zero-fee tier is real. The protocol rate binds only when the integrator fee is large enough that a quarter of it exceeds the schedule; below that, the protocol earns a quarter of the integrator's fee, which is a fifth of the total. And reopens are the thinnest line: 5 bps, a quarter of which is reserved, so the protocol earns under 4 bps net on a reopen no matter how large the venue's fee is.

The schedule matters only where the cap does not bind. At a 120 bps integrator fee a quarter of it is 30 bps, which is the proposed launch rate, so doubling the schedule changes nothing for that venue. At 300 bps a quarter is 75 bps, so the doubled launch rate of 60 does apply. The cap, not the schedule, decides what a low-fee venue pays.

## 7. Metered lines, and what the model cannot tell

The proposed rate card for managed services (the issuer subscription for recurring crosses, the listing fee scaled to the raise, per-call data). It prices a service around a permissionless program: anyone can still create a cross directly and pay only the protocol fee.

<!-- generated:metered -->
| Plan | Period | Price, USD | Crosses included | Each extra, USD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| recurring-starter | 30 days | 99.00 | 4 | 25.00 |
| recurring-weekly | 30 days | 399.00 | 22 | 15.00 |
<!-- /generated -->

The listing fee is 20 bps of the raise with a floor and a ceiling; pay-per-call data is priced per proof or export in the rate card. None of these prices comes from evidence of willingness to pay; they are a starting point and the first thing to test with real issuers.

What this document cannot establish, and what would:

1. **Volume.** Needs mainnet crosses with outside wallets (H8). The external-flow scorecard on the revenue page is the instrument; on devnet it reports zero outside wallets, which is the truth.
2. **Elasticity.** Whether a 30 bps launch fee or a 5 bps reopen fee changes how many issuers choose the cross. Needs a controlled comparison across venues that charge different integrator fees, which only exists once integrators exist.
3. **Integrator behaviour.** The integrator fee is the venue's choice. The model uses 20, 60, 120 and 300 bps as range markers, not as predictions.
4. **Cost to serve.** The break-even costs above are assumptions. The real costs (RPC, hosting, audit, support) belong in `BUILD.md` and are not there yet.

The decision this feeds is a human one: which schedule and rate card to publish (human task HD1 in `docs/HUMAN_TASKS.md`). The engineering is finished when the numbers are final: they are configuration of the protocol account and of the rate card, with no code change.
